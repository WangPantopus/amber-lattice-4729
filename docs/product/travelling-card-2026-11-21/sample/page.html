The travelling card

One Christmas card goes from home to home. Each person reads a note written only to them, adds their own for the next person, and sends the same card on. The envelope collects a postmark in every town. Press “Pass it on” and watch all six screens change.

How it would work

  • Passing it on is free. Starting a card uses a stamp, as sending one does today.
  • Every note has one reader. You read the note written to you. Earlier notes stay with the people they were written to.
  • The journey is public, people are not. The map and the postmarks show towns and dates. No names, no addresses, no notes.
  • Everyone who held the card hears each time it reaches another town, with the towns and miles so far.
  • No pressure. A card nobody passes on rests where it is. Nothing nags, and nothing bad is said to happen.

Still to decide

  • One next person, or more? If a holder may send it to two people, a card survives someone who never opens it, and it spreads faster.
  • First names on the journey page? Off in this sample. It could be each person’s own choice.
  • A shared goal. For example every state by Christmas morning, counted on one map for all cards.
  • Where a town comes from. A member’s place is known. Someone joining from a link would be asked for their town or ZIP code only.

The people here are made up. Towns, dates and miles are worked out on this page from real map positions.
